The View From the Top

What happens when you finally reach the top of the mountain you’ve spent so long trying to climb? What happens then?

We spend much of our lives chasing the next achievement, the next goal, the next dollar sign. Then we get there and find the horizon full of other peaks still to climb, other ideas to explore and put into action. If the summit was supposed to be the answer, why does it feel like another question?

Maybe it was never meant to be the answer. Things fade and go away. Trees lose their leaves every year, and the seasons change. The trophy gathers dust, the paycheck gets spent, and the milestone becomes something we used to talk about. Nature doesn’t count that as failure. It’s just how things move. The tree doesn’t mourn its leaves, because the leaves were never the tree.

The same goes for us. The achievement was never the point. The point was the climb, and the person it made you: the patience you learned, the fear you walked through, the strength you didn’t know you had. That stays with you after the view fades.

Look at the man on the rock. He isn’t planting a flag or looking for the next trail. He’s sitting still and watching the light come over the valley. That’s the part most of us skip. We reach the top and start scanning for the next mountain before we’ve taken a breath.

So here’s what happens at the summit, if we let it. We stop. We breathe. We look at how far we’ve come and remember who we are apart from what we’ve achieved. Then, from that center and not from fear or hunger, we choose the next peak.

The mountain was never the destination. You were.

~ Timothy
